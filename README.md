# Governance and Trust Vocabulary Commons

> **Meaningful discussion requires sufficiently precise, shared language.**

*An open vocabulary forum to collect and discuss terminology and meaning to populate a common curated glossary.*

## About the Commons

The Governance and Trust Vocabulary Commons is an open collection and collaborative reference for concepts, terminology, and relationships used to describe AI-enabled human, organizational, and technical environments.

Its purpose is to make emerging and established language discoverable, comparable, attributable, and open to critical examination. It distinguishes proposals from accepted definitions, documented behavior from hypotheses, and popularity from evidentiary validity.

The goal is to support the development of shared understanding without requiring agreement on terminology, interpretations, or governance prescriptions.

## How the Commons Will Work

The Commons is intended to support three complementary activities:

- **Collection:** Make existing and proposed terminology visible and attributable.
- **Discussion:** Compare definitions, challenge assumptions, examine evidence, and identify relationships.
- **Curation:** Maintain a structured glossary of definitions that have undergone documented review.

A submitted term need not be accepted into the curated glossary to remain visible and available for discussion.

## Participate in the Commons

There are several ways to contribute:

- **Join the discussion:** [Explore public Discussions](https://github.com/ai-governance-trust-commons/vocabulary-commons/discussions) to ask questions, compare definitions, examine evidence, and challenge interpretations.
- **Propose terminology:** [Submit a terminology proposal](https://github.com/ai-governance-trust-commons/vocabulary-commons/issues/new/choose) using our structured "Propose a Term" template.
- **Review the guidelines:** [Contributing to the Commons](CONTRIBUTING.md) explains provenance, evidence expectations, attribution, and editorial stewardship.
- **Understand editorial review:** [Read the Editorial Review and Curation Policy](EDITORIAL_REVIEW.md) to see how proposals are examined, decisions documented, and definitions reconsidered.

Participation is open. Publication of a proposal does not imply endorsement or acceptance into the curated glossary.

**We welcome contributors from every discipline and level of experience.** Help us identify established terminology, challenge definitions, contribute evidence, or clarify relationships. You do not need to arrive with a fully developed proposal.

## Principles for Participation

- Existing terminology should be recognized and respected, not replaced unnecessarily.
- Proposed terminology should clarify a meaningful distinction.
- Evidence and hypothesis must remain distinguishable.
- Popularity does not establish factual validity.
- Disagreement is an opportunity for examination, not a reason for exclusion.
- Changes to curated definitions require documented review.
