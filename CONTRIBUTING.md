# Contributing to the Governance and Trust Vocabulary Commons

> Meaningful discussion requires sufficiently precise, shared language.

Thank you for helping develop a common vocabulary for AI-enabled human, organizational, and technical environments.

The Commons welcomes proposed terms, alternative definitions, relevant research, corrections, and constructive disagreement.

Our purpose is shared understanding, not mandatory agreement or a new governance regime.

## 1. Proposing terminology

Before submitting a term, please search existing entries and discussions for similar concepts.

Open a GitHub Issue describing:

- **Term:** The proposed name or expression.
- **Definition:** What activity, condition, mechanism, relationship, or concept does it describe?
- **Distinction:** What does it clarify that existing terminology does not?
- **Provenance:** Where has the term or underlying concept appeared previously?
- **Related terms:** Which existing concepts overlap, differ, or connect?
- **Evidence:** Supporting references, observations, research, or examples, including relevant limitations.
- **Open questions:** What remains uncertain or disputed?

New terminology is not required when established language already describes the concept adequately.

## 2. Vocabulary and Claim descriptors

Each proposed entry should identify two independent attributes.

### Vocabulary status

- **Established:** Recognized terminology in existing usage.
- **Extended:** An established concept adapted or broadened.
- **Working:** A provisional term, analogy, or descriptive construct.

### Claim standing

- **Foundation:** Conceptual or theoretical basis, not an empirical finding.
- **Demonstrated (bounded):** Behavior documented under specified conditions.
- **Indicated:** Supported by observations or inference but not established generally.
- **Hypothesis:** A broader proposition requiring further investigation.

**Normative proposals** must be identified separately from empirical claims.

A proposed term does not establish that its underlying phenomenon exists. Likewise, widespread use of terminology does not establish factual validity.

## 3. Discussion and review

Submissions may be:

- Proposed and awaiting discussion.
- Under examination or revision.
- Incorporated into the curated glossary.
- Retained as alternatives, disputed proposals, or historical references.

Publication of a proposal does not imply acceptance or endorsement.

Contributors are encouraged to challenge definitions, question classifications, identify equivalent terminology, and offer contradictory evidence.

Disagreement is not grounds for exclusion.

## 4. Editorial stewardship and review

The Commons begins with a founding steward responsible for maintaining the repository, preserving the published baseline, and facilitating contributions.

Stewardship does not confer permanent or exclusive authority over terminology, definitions, or their interpretation.

Editorial decisions should develop through transparent, documented review, with participation from independent contributors and subject-matter reviewers as the Commons grows.

During its initial development, the founding steward may maintain and approve changes, subject to the following principles:

- Proposed terminology and alternative definitions remain visible even when not accepted into the curated glossary.
- Decisions should explain their rationale, including relevant evidence, conceptual distinctions, and unresolved disagreements.
- Contributors may challenge decisions and request reconsideration.
- Materially disputed changes should receive independent review when qualified reviewers are available.
- Editorial responsibilities and decision authority should become distributed as participation and organizational maturity permit.

Accepted revisions must preserve attribution and version history.

AI systems may support collection, analysis, evaluation, and editorial preparation, but may not independently authorize changes to the curated reference.

The objective is responsible curation, not control over which ideas may be expressed or examined.

## 5. Attribution and licensing

Contributors must identify relevant original sources and respect third-party intellectual property.

Original written contributions submitted for inclusion in the Commons are intended for publication under the repository's CC BY 4.0 content license. By contributing such material, contributors agree to that licensing and confirm they have the necessary rights.

AI-assisted submissions are welcome, but contributors remain responsible for reviewing factual claims, verifying references, and identifying meaningful uncertainty.
