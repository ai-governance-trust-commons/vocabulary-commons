Exploring relationships over time

When do separate events connect?

Investigate what really links an earlier activity to a later outcome, even when the actors, tools, or organizations differ.

← Return to Explore questions

Exploratory question · Not a new glossary term

What connects the stages?

An incident can leave information, permissions or dependencies that affect a later actor—even after the original task ends. But a sequence of events is not automatically a causal chain. The question is what passed between stages, whether the next actor actually used it, and what changed as a result.

01

Unintended continuation

An earlier activity leaves a condition that someone later relies on without the originator intending the eventual outcome.

02

Deliberately staged continuation

An actor prepares a note, instruction or authorization for later use. The attempt can still fail before activation.

03

Opportunistic continuation

A later actor deliberately exploits a condition left by someone else, without any demonstrated shared plan.

Questions before calling events connected

  1. What identifiable carrier connects the stages: memory, document, credential, authority or another state?
  2. Is the later actor's access, reliance or action observed, or only possible?
  3. Which boundary was crossed, and what triggered the next step?
  4. Whose intention is documented? Causation does not imply coordination or a shared objective.
  5. Which link failed, was interrupted or has an alternative explanation?

Starting points in published evidence

  • EVID-108 · Successor memory

    A controlled predecessor-to-successor experiment; storing a note did not guarantee its later use.

  • EVID-180 · Email-assistant propagation

    A constructed ecosystem demonstrating propagation with application-controlled routing.

  • EVID-195 · Delegated authority after shutdown

    Reported surviving authorization; not evidence that an AI agent kept operating.

  • EVID-141 · Dependent mathematical work

    Correction and dependency questions around reused arguments; the agent-to-agent transmission path has not been established.

Status and limits: This is a question for continuing analysis, not a finding that all four cases form one mechanism. “Event-chain continuity” is an exploratory analytical label, not part of the 35-term founding glossary. Chronology, repeated citations and shared glossary annotations alone do not prove causal transmission, independent corroboration, recursive feedback or deliberate collusion.
