Research path 01 / 8 · Human oversight

When is human oversight more than an approval step?

Separate who makes a decision from who can recognize, assess and correct it—and whether there is time to act.

Keep the comparison bounded.

A taxonomy does not demonstrate effective oversight. A short simulated task does not establish field takeover performance or long-term competence.

Questions to carry into the reading

  1. Does human input constitute the decision, or provide an opportunity to correct it?
  2. What do the measurements establish about awareness, response time and intervention?
  3. Which parts are conceptual distinctions, and which were observed in a task?

Reading 1 · EVID-110 · Normative proposal

Distinguish decision-making from correction

Why this is selected — Commons editorial note: Start with a proposed causal taxonomy that separates different kinds of human involvement.

Finding as recorded in the Library: Authors distinguish humans whose input constitutes a decision from humans able to correct or prevent it, with temporal subtypes of corrective involvement.

Limits to retain: Proposed causal taxonomy, not evidence that a named oversight arrangement works. Runtime scope and role distinctions need operational validation; no founding Commons definition change follows from retaining this comparison.

Reading 2 · EVID-176 · Reported observation

Examine response after automation is withdrawn

Why this is selected — Commons editorial note: Then compare that vocabulary with a bounded experiment measuring later manual responses. The task and timing assumptions matter.

Finding as recorded in the Library: Source-reported finding: Eighty undergraduates completed a simulated navigation task across five automation conditions. After assistance was withdrawn, final-scenario response times differed across conditions, including a significant manual-versus-fully-automated contrast. Accuracy and workload did not significantly differ; confidence did not decline as hypothesized. Observation period and measurement context: Brief simulated task exposure; experimental calendar dates not established.

Limits to retain: Student sample, simplified task, short exposure, explicit failure and advance warning. The programmed 30-second monitoring/veto interval is not observed takeover latency. Exact plotted means were not reliably extracted. Not proof of skill decay or one unique mechanism.
