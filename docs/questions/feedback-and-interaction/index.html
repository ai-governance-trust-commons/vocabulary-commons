Questions to carry into the reading

  1. What passes from one activity into another, and what comes back?
  2. Is the result faster activity, amplified effects, or a changed distribution?
  3. Under what assumptions might feedback stabilize rather than amplify instability?

Reading 1 · EVID-166 · Reported observation

Trace a recommender feedback mechanism

Why this is selected — Commons editorial note: Simulation shows how recommendations can shape later training data and homogenize consumption without matching utility gains.

Finding as recorded in the Library: Source-reported finding: In simulations, retraining recommenders on behavior shaped by earlier recommendations increases homogenization without corresponding utility improvement and changes consumption distributions. Observation period and measurement context: Discrete simulation steps with synthetic users/items; no real-world observation dates.

Limits to retain: Simulated preferences, selected algorithms and one interaction per user per step limit transfer. Effects vary by algorithm; random recommendations provide a contrast. Population-level polarization was not measured.

Reading 2 · EVID-167 · Conceptual framing

Compare conditions for convergence

Why this is selected — Commons editorial note: A formal analysis offers a boundary to inevitable-instability claims. Stability itself is not a judgment of desirability.

Finding as recorded in the Library: Source-reported finding: The authors establish conditional convergence to a stable model when the induced distribution and loss meet stated sensitivity, smoothness and convexity assumptions. Counterexamples diverge when assumptions fail. Observation period and measurement context: Formal analysis and illustrative experiments; no longitudinal field sample.

Limits to retain: These assumptions need not hold in deployed ecosystems. Stability does not imply social desirability or global optimality. Proofs and code were not independently checked by the Commons.

Reading 3 · EVID-180 · Reported observation

Examine propagation in a constructed ecosystem

Why this is selected — Commons editorial note: The email-assistant demonstration makes the application’s recipient choices a crucial boundary on what the prompt controls.

Finding as recorded in the Library: Source-reported finding: Cohen and colleagues demonstrate prompt propagation and confidential-data extraction in a constructed retrieval-based email-assistant ecosystem.

Limits to retain: The prompt changes generated content; the application determines recipients. This is not evidence of arbitrary autonomous recipient selection or an uncontrolled outbreak. The revised paper also evaluates a guardrail; adaptive attacks on that guardrail were not tested.
