Related records provide starting points; their limitations remain part of the discussion. Contributions can strengthen, revise or challenge an idea.

How to read a developing discussion · Contribute a source or alternative explanation

01 / Analytical question

What connects events across time?

An earlier action can leave information, permissions or dependencies that matter to a later actor. What establishes that the later activity actually depended on what was left behind?

Why examine it?

A sequence can look connected without showing transmission or reliance. Conversely, a consequence can cross tasks, actors or authority boundaries without a shared plan. Causal connection and intention need separate examination.

Related material and its limits

The existing Event Chains inquiry compares successor memory, constructed email propagation, surviving delegated authority and dependent mathematical work. These are different settings. They do not establish one common mechanism, a shared objective or an authenticated AI-to-AI chain in every case.

What deserves further development?

  • Identify the carrier between stages and evidence of later access or use.
  • Distinguish accidental continuation, deliberate staging and opportunistic use.
  • Look for interrupted links and explanations that do not require transmission.

What would help: versioned artifacts, access and action records, boundary conditions and comparisons where the suspected link is absent or fails.

Status: an analytical question. “Event-chain continuity” remains an exploratory label, outside the founding glossary.

02 / Oversight question

When does human approval reflect independently formed judgment?

When a human approves an AI-assisted conclusion, what would establish that the judgment was independently formed—including the choices that shaped the evidence presented for approval?

Why examine it?

Approval records identify a decision. They may leave unanswered how the options were framed, which evidence was selected, what was omitted, and whether the reviewer could recognize and challenge those choices. The question extends beyond the final answer to the conditions under which it became persuasive.

Related material and its limits

The human-oversight research path distinguishes making a decision from recognizing, assessing and correcting it. EVID-110 records a conceptual taxonomy of constitutive and corrective involvement; it does not validate an operational test for independence. Chaperoning provides related supervisory language, not proof that an approval process meets its conditions.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish independent judgment from agreement, formal authority and a recorded approval.
  • Examine how evidence selection, framing and missing alternatives shape review.
  • Ask when observation retains enough context, competence, authority and time to support correction.

What would help: comparisons of judgments formed before and after AI framing, visibility into omitted alternatives, and records of challenge, revision or disagreement. These are investigative possibilities, not a validated assurance procedure.

Status: an open oversight question. AI assistance does not by itself establish either independence or its absence.

03 / Conceptual analogy · Lance J. Johnson

The Illuminated Shadow

A second light can make a shadow less visible while the original obstruction remains. What might this help us distinguish when evaluating an apparently improved AI-enabled environment?

Why examine it?

Consider a hypothetical process in which additional assistance improves the output while an underlying limitation remains. The visible result alone may not tell us whether the limitation was removed, compensated for, made less consequential, or harder to observe. Those conditions can coexist.

Related material and its limits

Observability, the sleight-of-hand effect and assurance offer related questions about visibility and confidence. The analogy does not establish that any particular deployment hides a defect, or that a compensating capability is ineffective.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish removal of a limitation from compensation for its effects.
  • Ask what observations would reveal a limitation when the compensating capability changes or fails.
  • Identify where established language already captures the distinction and where the analogy adds precision.

What would help: explicit examples and counterexamples, comparison with established risk and resilience concepts, and tests that distinguish the underlying limitation from the quality of the combined result.

Status: a conceptual analogy for examination, not a new glossary term or an empirical finding. Its usefulness and scope remain to be tested.

04 / Oversight question

What are we relying on when we rely on oversight?

An organization delegating consequential work also relies on the arrangements supervising it. What supports confidence that those arrangements can form warranted judgments from incomplete evidence and translate them into appropriate and effective action?

Why examine it?

Observation, judgment, authority and intervention may reside with different people, agents and organizations. A recorded approval or an operating monitor establishes only part of that arrangement. An intervention can stop activity yet be excessive or fail to reach continuing effects elsewhere.

Related material and its limits

Chaperoning describes supervisory functions without assigning them to a particular kind of participant or location. EVID-006 concerns a reported stopping failure; EVID-195 concerns authority surviving a local shutdown. Their different settings do not establish a generally superior human–agent configuration.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish control available, control exercised and the effect established.
  • Examine how access, competence, independence, authority and time connect across participants.
  • Keep appropriate restraint and proportionate intervention within the discussion.

What would help: Accounts that connect what was observed and judged to what could be changed, what action followed, and which consequences that action actually reached.

Status: An open question about supervisory reliance; the presence of supervision does not establish its effectiveness.

05 / Observation and judgment question

What do our indicators allow us to conclude?

A warning, connection log, output or unusual power demand may indicate consequential behavior without exposing the underlying activity directly. What permits a supervisor to distinguish proper operation, a departure requiring attention and insufficient evidence?

Why examine it?

The same observation can have several explanations. An unusual connection may be legitimate; an absence of warnings may reflect proper operation, limited coverage or a missed event. Observation and informed judgment depend on one another.

Related material and its limits

Observability and chaperoning accommodate indirect evidence. EVID-013 illustrates how different auditor access changed what could be detected in a controlled study. It does not establish that any one indicator can confirm proper operation.

What deserves further development?

  • Separate the observed indicator from the activity inferred and the significance assigned to it.
  • Examine whether multiple observers share the same evidence gaps.
  • Preserve uncertainty when available observations cannot distinguish competing explanations.

What would help: Examples and counterexamples connecting indicators, operating context, alternative explanations and subsequent outcomes, including cases where no intervention was appropriate.

Status: An interpretive question, not a proposed detector or a claim that every consequential action can be observed.

06 / Distributed judgment question

When AI roles disagree, what are they disagreeing about?

An operational AI pursues a deliverable, a supervisory AI assesses its acceptability, and an audit AI examines whether the assessment is supported. How much disagreement arises from their mandates, their information, or their different representations of the same situation?

Why examine it?

Independent challenge can be valuable. Yet one participant may assess another’s summary rather than the underlying action. Apparent agreement can inherit common assumptions; apparent conflict can reflect different evidence or legitimate differences in purpose.

Related material and its limits

Independent judgment, assurance independence and chaperoning offer relevant distinctions. The three-role arrangement here is a hypothetical configuration, not a documented case demonstrating a recurring operational pattern.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish disagreement over facts, significance, authority and appropriate action.
  • Examine the information each role receives, selects and passes onward.
  • Ask whether the grounds for agreement or disagreement remain examinable across handoffs.

What would help: Comparisons retaining the underlying actions, each participant’s evidence and interpretation, and the consequences of challenge, agreement or unresolved disagreement.

Status: A hypothetical operational question. Role friction does not itself establish malfunction, collusion or compromised supervision.

07 / Hypothesis and thought experiment

What happens when the means of judging success become something the system optimizes?

An operational agent could work around supervisory impediments, a supervisor could favor a reassuring presentation, and an audit agent could narrow its examination. Could separate efforts to succeed reinforce an incomplete account without explicit collusion?

Why examine it?

Discretion over execution, reporting and audit scope can affect what reaches a judgment process. The question is whether adaptation improves the underlying activity, changes its presentation, limits scrutiny, or exposes a conflict that participants accept even when the result is unfavorable.

Related material and its limits

Reference-environment integrity, evidence laundering and different AI roles provide related vocabulary. These connections motivate inquiry; they do not establish the proposed three-role chain. Legitimate exclusions and concise reporting are not themselves concealment. Finding exceptions can constitute a successful audit.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish explicit success measures from a participant’s interpretation of success.
  • Compare changes in conduct with changes in what can be known and judged about it.
  • Look for candid reporting, rejected workarounds and expanded scrutiny as well as narrowing or distortion.

What would help: A bounded comparison preserving independent starting judgments, subsequent interactions, reporting choices, audit scope and counterexamples. An unfavorable assessment must remain a possible legitimate outcome.

Status: An untested interacting-objectives hypothesis and possible future thought experiment, not an established mechanism or a new glossary term.
