How to read a developing discussion · Contribute a source or alternative explanation

01 / Analytical question

What connects events across time?

An earlier action can leave information, permissions or dependencies that matter to a later actor. What establishes that the later activity actually depended on what was left behind?

Why examine it?

A sequence can look connected without showing transmission or reliance. Conversely, a consequence can cross tasks, actors or authority boundaries without a shared plan. Causal connection and intention need separate examination.

Related material and its limits

The existing Event Chains inquiry compares successor memory, constructed email propagation, surviving delegated authority and dependent mathematical work. These are different settings. They do not establish one common mechanism, a shared objective or an authenticated AI-to-AI chain in every case.

What deserves further development?

  • Identify the carrier between stages and evidence of later access or use.
  • Distinguish accidental continuation, deliberate staging and opportunistic use.
  • Look for interrupted links and explanations that do not require transmission.

What would help: versioned artifacts, access and action records, boundary conditions and comparisons where the suspected link is absent or fails.

Status: an analytical question. “Event-chain continuity” remains an exploratory label, outside the founding glossary.

02 / Oversight question

When does human approval reflect independently formed judgment?

When a human approves an AI-assisted conclusion, what would establish that the judgment was independently formed—including the choices that shaped the evidence presented for approval?

Why examine it?

Approval records identify a decision. They may leave unanswered how the options were framed, which evidence was selected, what was omitted, and whether the reviewer could recognize and challenge those choices. The question extends beyond the final answer to the conditions under which it became persuasive.

Related material and its limits

The human-oversight research path distinguishes making a decision from recognizing, assessing and correcting it. EVID-110 records a conceptual taxonomy of constitutive and corrective involvement; it does not validate an operational test for independence. Chaperoning provides related supervisory language, not proof that an approval process meets its conditions.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish independent judgment from agreement, formal authority and a recorded approval.
  • Examine how evidence selection, framing and missing alternatives shape review.
  • Ask when observation retains enough context, competence, authority and time to support correction.

What would help: comparisons of judgments formed before and after AI framing, visibility into omitted alternatives, and records of challenge, revision or disagreement. These are investigative possibilities, not a validated assurance procedure.

Status: an open oversight question. AI assistance does not by itself establish either independence or its absence.

03 / Conceptual analogy · Lance J. Johnson

The Illuminated Shadow

A second light can make a shadow less visible while the original obstruction remains. What might this help us distinguish when evaluating an apparently improved AI-enabled environment?

Why examine it?

Consider a hypothetical process in which additional assistance improves the output while an underlying limitation remains. The visible result alone may not tell us whether the limitation was removed, compensated for, made less consequential, or harder to observe. Those conditions can coexist.

Related material and its limits

Observability, the sleight-of-hand effect and assurance offer related questions about visibility and confidence. The analogy does not establish that any particular deployment hides a defect, or that a compensating capability is ineffective.

What deserves further development?

  • Distinguish removal of a limitation from compensation for its effects.
  • Ask what observations would reveal a limitation when the compensating capability changes or fails.
  • Identify where established language already captures the distinction and where the analogy adds precision.

What would help: explicit examples and counterexamples, comparison with established risk and resilience concepts, and tests that distinguish the underlying limitation from the quality of the combined result.

Status: a conceptual analogy for examination, not a new glossary term or an empirical finding. Its usefulness and scope remain to be tested.

A contribution can be valuable because it shows that an idea is unnecessary, misleading or already described more precisely elsewhere.
