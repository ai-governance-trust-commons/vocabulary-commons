01 / Collection

Standards & institutions

Established standards, institutional guidance, and regulatory reference points. These documents are external to the Commons and have different purposes and legal standing.

  • External resource · NIST · voluntary framework

    NIST AI Risk Management Framework ↗

    A widely used risk-management reference for organizations developing or using AI; a useful point of comparison, not the Commons' framework.

  • External resource · NIST · sector-neutral guidance

    NIST Generative AI Profile (AI 600-1) ↗

    Extends the AI RMF's risk considerations to generative AI, including characteristics of AI-enabled operating environments.

  • External resource · ISO / IEC · international standard

    ISO/IEC 42001 — AI management systems ↗

    Requirements for an organizational AI management system; distinguishes management responsibilities from descriptive research questions.

  • External resource · OECD · intergovernmental recommendation

    OECD AI Principles ↗

    International reference principles on trustworthy AI, accountability, transparency, robustness and human-centered governance.

  • External resource · European Commission · regulatory reference

    European Commission — AI Act overview ↗

    Official explanation of the EU AI Act, its obligations and application timeline. Consult the legal text for binding requirements.

  • External resource · Japan METI & MIC · national AI governance guidance

    Japan METI / MIC — AI Guidelines for Business v1.2 ↗

    Japan’s March 2026 AI business guidelines distinguish responsibilities across developers, providers and users. The official page includes English translations; these are non-binding guidance, not Commons standards.

  • External resource · OWASP · security community guidance

    OWASP GenAI LLM Top 10 (2026) ↗

    Current community-maintained application-security risks and mitigations for LLM systems; complements governance descriptions.

  • External resource · NIST AI 100-2e2025 · technical terminology

    NIST Adversarial ML Taxonomy (2025) ↗

    A technical language for adversarial AI methods, attacker goals and mitigations; useful when distinguishing mechanism from speculation.

02 / Collection

Research & publications

Selected reports and original studies relevant to capability, reliance, cross-agent effects, assurance and incident reconstruction. Experimental findings retain their study-specific limits.

  • External resource · Stanford HAI · research synthesis

    Stanford AI Index Report 2026 ↗

    A broad evidence-oriented snapshot of AI development, adoption, technical performance and governance trends.

  • External resource · METR · capability measurement

    Task-Completion Time Horizons ↗

    Measurements of frontier agents' software-task time horizons; informative for capability and intervention-window questions, not a measure of all work.

  • External resource · Cemri et al. · research paper

    Why Do Multi-Agent LLM Systems Fail? ↗

    Analyzes failure patterns across multi-agent systems, including coordination, specification and verification limits.

  • External resource · Ezell, Roberts-Gaal & Chan · research paper

    Incident Analysis for AI Agents ↗

    A proposal for reconstructing agent incidents from system, context and activity evidence; a methodological comparator, not an adopted Commons framework.

  • External resource · Das et al. · controlled study

    Self-Propagating Misalignment in LLM Agents ↗

    Tests whether agent-written memory or files affect a successor; controlled setups and failed transfers matter to interpreting persistence.

  • External resource · Xu et al. · controlled benchmark

    After the Attacker Leaves: Carryover Attacks ↗

    Separates planting from later triggering via persistent state and scheduled tasks. Manual test wakes do not establish unattended deployment prevalence.

  • External resource · Yan et al. · controlled multi-agent study

    When Truth Is Distributed ↗

    Examines how false testimony affects collective fact recovery and how a claim may spread after its original source exits.

04 / Collection

Related initiatives

Independent collections and research organizations that offer different lenses on AI incidents, security, safety and governance. Inclusion does not mean methodological agreement.

  • External resource · Responsible AI Collaborative · incident collection

    AI Incident Database ↗

    Searchable reporting on AI harms and near harms. Incident records may depend on the same underlying reporting sources.

  • External resource · MIT · risk taxonomy and database

    MIT AI Risk Repository ↗

    A cross-framework inventory of AI risks with causal and domain classifications; helpful for comparing terminology and coverage.

  • External resource · MITRE · adversarial AI knowledge base

    MITRE ATLAS ↗

    Documented AI-related adversary tactics and techniques drawn from observed attacks and realistic demonstrations.

  • External resource · OECD.AI · automated incident monitor (beta)

    OECD AI Incidents and Hazards Monitor ↗

    Tracks media-reported incidents and hazards using automated aggregation; review original reporting before treating entries as verified events.

  • External resource · UK government · evaluation and research

    UK AI Security Institute ↗

    Research and evaluations concerning the risks and capabilities of advanced AI systems, with separate institutional aims from the Commons.

Selection and maintenance

Curated, not comprehensive.

Inclusion requires an identifiable publisher, a direct and useful source, and a clear connection to the Commons’ descriptive mission. We favor original sources and preserve distinctions between standards, proposals, controlled experiments, attributed incident reports, and independently verified evidence.

Listing is not endorsement, independent verification, or acceptance of a source’s conclusions. External sites, their versions, terms and access may change. Links should be reviewed when the Commons is updated or a correction is reported; superseded items should be revised or withdrawn with an editorial explanation.

For individual observations, sources and limitations, use the Evidence Library and its publication policy. Suggestions and corrections are welcome through Participate.
