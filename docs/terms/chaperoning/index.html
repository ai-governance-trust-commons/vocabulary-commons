Chaperoning

Vocabulary · WorkingClaim · Foundation

Definition

The continuing supervisory activity of independently observing consequential behavior, exercising informed judgment, and intervening effectively when appropriate. This requires access, competence, authority, and an actual ability to alter outcomes—not nominal participation or an agent merely stating that an action is inappropriate.

Relationship to other concepts

Meaningful human control is a condition chaperoning may help support. BVR can hide which activity warrants attention; horizon compression can remove intervention time; trajectory persistence can make a supervised agent less responsive even when a problem is identified. Trajectory persistence does not imply that a chaperone cannot enforce an external constraint; a control air gap can separate the agent’s continued intent from its authority to execute.
