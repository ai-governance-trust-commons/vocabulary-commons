Meaningful human control

Vocabulary · EstablishedClaim · Foundation

Definition

A condition in which people retain adequate information, independent judgment, authority, and practical means to redirect consequential activity. A nominal human-in-the-loop approval does not, by itself, demonstrate this capacity.

Relationship to other concepts

Chaperoning describes active supervision that can help produce meaningful human control. Horizon compression limits time for intervention; trajectory persistence can impair an agent’s uptake of corrective signals, which is distinct from removing an external human’s intervention authority.
