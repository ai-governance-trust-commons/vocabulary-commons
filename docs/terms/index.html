The founding reference

Browse the vocabulary

Thirty-five alphabetical entries from the fixed v1.0.2 glossary. Each term retains its original definition, relationship, and status; linked terms reveal nearby concepts.

35 terms

  • AI-enabled capability

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    An organizational function delivered through an AI model and the people, tools, data, workflows, and dependencies required to produce its intended outcome. The capability—not only the model—is the relevant unit when discussing operational consequences.

  • Assurance

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    Evidence-supported confidence that specified properties or outcomes will hold under stated objectives, operating conditions, and assumptions. Assurance has a defined scope and can lose relevance when those conditions change.

  • Assurance independence

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    An evaluator’s practical freedom to obtain necessary evidence, choose evaluation methods, form judgments, and report findings without undue control by the actor or system being evaluated. Separate organizational status alone is insufficient.

  • Attacker-model change

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    A material shift in what adversaries can feasibly attempt, given available tools, knowledge, resources, speed, and scale. Exposure can increase even when the protected system and its controls remain unchanged.

  • Beyond visual range (BVR)

    →
    WorkingIndicated

    A condition in which consequential actions, effects, or causal relationships fall outside what an observer recognizes as relevant to the process being examined. Those activities may be visible elsewhere, yet the connection that makes them consequential is unrecognized. Neither concealment nor a defective local monitor is required.

  • Capability feasibility progression

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    An analytical sequence for describing distinct levels of practical possibility: possible → practical → repeatable → scalable. A step need not follow the preceding one automatically, and the categories are not a universal maturity model.

  • Chaperoning

    →
    WorkingFoundation

    The continuing supervisory activity of independently observing consequential behavior, exercising informed judgment, and intervening effectively when appropriate. This requires access, competence, authority, and an actual ability to alter outcomes—not nominal participation or an agent merely stating that an action is inappropriate.

  • Competency decay

    →
    ExtendedIndicated

    A decline in the human or organizational expertise needed to perform, verify, contest, or recover work that is repeatedly delegated to AI-enabled systems. It is especially consequential when skills needed for independent judgment are lost.

  • Constraint compression

    →
    WorkingDemonstrated (bounded)

    A reduction, often AI-enabled, in the practical barriers of knowledge, expertise, labor, cost, time, or coordination that once limited an activity. It describes the change in constraints, not the eventual impact of the activity.

  • Control air gap

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    An architectural separation between an agent’s ability to recommend, select, or plan an action and the authority or mechanism that permits consequential execution. Independent enforcement can maintain the separation even when the agent’s internal reasoning changes.

  • Cross-system feedback

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    A pattern in which an action or output from one technical or organizational process affects activity elsewhere and subsequent responses modify conditions for additional participants. Many response paths may exist; there need not be a single linear loop.

  • Demonstrated behavior

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    An observed, reproduced, or reliably reconstructed action or mechanism in a specified setting. Demonstration establishes that the behavior occurred under those conditions, not its frequency or generality.

  • Distributed adaptive control environment

    →
    WorkingHypothesis

    A proposed characterization of interacting human, organizational, and AI activities in which participants with distinct objectives continuously influence one another’s information, actions, and relationships. Its usefulness is descriptive; the scope and consequences of such environments require investigation.

  • Environmental redirection

    →
    ExtendedDemonstrated (bounded)

    A change in an agent’s actions, methods, beliefs, or priorities arising from information, artifacts, tools, constraints, or incentives encountered while performing a task. Such redirection can be beneficial or harmful; an altered belief, selected method, and adopted objective are not the same outcome.

  • Evidence laundering through AI

    →
    WorkingIndicated

    The appearance of credibility or corroboration acquired by low-integrity material when AI systems retrieve, summarize, cite, or re-present it. Multiple apparently agreeing outputs may share the same original, manipulated source.

  • Feedback acceleration

    →
    ExtendedIndicated

    The shortening of intervals between consequential activities, responses, and later adjustments in an interacting process. It describes the tempo of the process, whether or not the number or severity of responses increases.

  • Feedback amplification

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    A feedback effect in which an activity and the reactions it generates increase the intensity, reach, amount, or consequences of subsequent activity. Stabilizing or dampening responses may limit or reverse amplification.

  • Governability

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    A property of a consequential process describing the extent to which legitimate parties can understand, influence, constrain, correct, or recover its operation and outcomes. It does not imply complete predictability or a single controller.

  • Horizon compression

    →
    ExtendedIndicated

    A process in which the time available to identify, interpret, decide about, and intervene in consequential activity becomes shorter relative to the time intervention requires. It is an intervention-window problem, not a general label for every obstacle to correcting an agent.

  • Human–AI mutual adaptation

    →
    ExtendedIndicated

    Reciprocal changes in human practices and AI-enabled activities as each responds to the other. Delegation can change how people decide and work; human behavior and institutions can reshape AI adoption, incentives, and subsequent capabilities.

  • Investigative indicator

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    An observed characteristic or combination of signals that provides a reason to investigate a possible event, mechanism, or hidden relationship without establishing that it occurred.

  • Meaningful human control

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    A condition in which people retain adequate information, independent judgment, authority, and practical means to redirect consequential activity. A nominal human-in-the-loop approval does not, by itself, demonstrate this capacity.

  • Mechanism hypothesis

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    A provisional explanation specifying how certain conditions, activities, or relationships could produce an observed or anticipated effect. A useful hypothesis names evidence that could support, qualify, or weaken the explanation.

  • Observability

    →
    EstablishedFoundation

    The capacity to obtain and interpret dependable evidence about states, actions, relationships, and consequences relevant to a specified question. Evidence may come from more than one system or organization.

  • Observability boundary

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    The practical limit of what a given observer or monitoring arrangement can detect, interpret, or causally connect. The limit may be known, partly understood, or itself unrecognized.

  • Operational trust

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    Reliance placed in an AI-enabled capability through actual use, decisions, and delegation, whether or not users express subjective confidence in it. Trust in this sense is operational behavior, not a reliability certification.

  • Organizational dependency

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    Material reliance by an organization on an AI-enabled capability to conduct operations, make decisions, or sustain service. Consequences depend on alternatives, retained expertise, and capacity to continue or recover without the capability.

  • Outcome–purpose divergence

    →
    ExtendedDemonstrated (bounded)

    A condition in which meeting an immediate objective or metric degrades the benefit or purpose that justified pursuing it. The divergence can occur through narrow measures or indirect consequences without an intent to deceive.

  • Purpose preservation

    →
    WorkingEntry: Normative proposal

    A proposed principle that the achievement of an objective should not destroy or irreversibly undermine the conditions giving it intended value to its beneficiaries. It tests the meaning of success, not solely performance against a target.

  • Purposeful continuity

    →
    WorkingEntry: Normative proposal

    A proposed principle for evolving AI capabilities and successors: changes in methods, objectives, or architecture should preserve defensible connections to intended value and permit independent reconsideration. This remains a normative proposal, not a demonstrated alignment solution.

  • Reference environment integrity

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    The reliability, provenance, completeness, and resistance to manipulation of information used to make operational or assurance judgments, including documents, logs, telemetry, evidence, and monitoring outputs.

  • Sleight-of-hand effect

    →
    WorkingFoundation

    An analogy for a mismatch between apparently successful local observation and the consequential process actually unfolding. An observer may see what was assigned for monitoring while missing actions or relationships elsewhere that change the outcome. Deliberate concealment is not required.

  • Successor assurance

    →
    ExtendedFoundation

    Evaluation of whether a significantly modified or AI-created successor preserves specified behavioral, authorization, evidence, control, and purpose-related properties. Passing performance tests alone does not demonstrate preservation of these properties.

  • System-level autonomy

    →
    WorkingHypothesis

    A possible condition in which interactions among humans, organizations, and AI systems sustain consequential patterns no single participant meaningfully directs or can correct. It is a claim about collective operation, not necessarily autonomy of any one agent.

  • Trajectory persistence

    →
    WorkingDemonstrated (bounded)

    A tendency for an agent to continue an established approach despite information suggesting the approach is inappropriate, unsafe, or outside scope. In controlled incident replays, continued behavior was observed. The explanation that prior actions and compaction summaries create a self-reinforcing “momentum” remains a researcher hypothesis, not a verified causal mechanism [8–10].
