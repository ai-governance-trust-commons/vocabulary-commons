Editorial Review and Curation

Governance and Trust Vocabulary Commons
Working editorial guidance — October 2026

Meaningful discussion requires sufficiently precise, shared language.

Purpose

The Commons is a repository in which terminology describing AI-enabled human, organizational, and technical environments is collected and curated through collaborative examination and documented editorial agreement.

Its editorial process exists to improve shared understanding and establish a common lexicon, not to establish exclusive authority over meaning or determine which ideas may be expressed.

The Commons evaluates proposed terminology and definitions on their merits, supports their collaborative development, and treats accepted definitions as revisable expressions of current understanding rather than institutional declarations of truth.

1. Equal Consideration

All contributions receive genuine and equivalent consideration, regardless of the contributor's credentials, affiliation, reputation, experience, or familiarity with formal research and publication practices.

Competing proposals must be examined against consistent criteria. Differences in evidentiary support determine the sufficiency of a claim, not whether the contribution merits examination.

Consideration includes clarity, relevance, distinction from existing terminology, provenance, available evidence, and potential usefulness.

An unfamiliar or imperfectly expressed contribution must not be dismissed solely because of its presentation. Clarification or collaborative development may be appropriate.

2. Collaborative Development

A contributor is not required to possess all the evidence or expertise necessary to substantiate an observation or proposed concept.

Other participants, including reviewers, may identify prior research, clarify definitions, develop examples, examine alternative interpretations, or supply supporting and contradictory evidence.

The Commons must distinguish and appropriately attribute the original contribution and subsequent substantive development.

Initial submissions may be incomplete. Supporting evidence and refinements may be contributed during examination by participants other than the original submitter.

3. Evidence and Intellectual Discipline

Editorial examination distinguishes among:

  • The provenance and standing of terminology: Established, Extended, or Working.
  • The evidentiary standing of associated claims: Foundation, Demonstrated (bounded), Indicated, or Hypothesis.
  • Normative proposals, which remain distinguishable from descriptive or empirical claims.

Definitions and explanations of these descriptors are available in the founding glossary's classification guide.

Incomplete evidence does not automatically disqualify a potentially useful concept. However, claims must not be represented as more established, complete, or general than their supporting evidence warrants.

Available contradictory evidence and relevant alternative explanations must be examined. Material unresolved questions, evidentiary limitations, and disagreements must be documented.

4. Review and Disposition

GitHub Issues provide a public record of submissions and examination. Workflow labels communicate progress without constituting judgments of validity.

The Commons uses five statuses:

  • Proposed: Submitted for consideration.
  • Under review: Being examined or developed.
  • Needs further evidence: Material clarification or support remains outstanding.
  • Incorporated: Included in the curated glossary following documented review.
  • Retained for discussion: Not incorporated, but preserved as an alternative, disputed interpretation, or historical contribution.

These statuses do not represent a mandatory sequence. A proposal may move between statuses or be reconsidered as additional support or new understanding emerges.

5. Editorial Decisions

The extent of review must be proportionate to the significance, complexity, and uncertainty of the contribution.

For substantive editorial decisions, the public record must identify the decision, its principal rationale, relevant evidence or conceptual distinctions, and material unresolved disagreements.

Reviewers may assist in developing a contribution without endorsing its conclusions.

The founding steward coordinates initial maintenance and editorial decisions. That responsibility does not confer permanent or exclusive authority over terminology, definitions, inclusion in the curated glossary, or their interpretation.

For materially disputed decisions, editors will seek additional perspectives where practical and document significant unresolved disagreements. A decision does not require unanimity and remains open to reconsideration when further information becomes available.

Automated tools, including AI agents, may support any part of the examination process, including research, identifying and verifying citations, comparing definitions, evaluating evidence, drafting, and recommending classifications. Such assistance remains subject to human review.

Final decisions concerning a submission's editorial status and inclusion in the curated glossary require authorization by responsible human editors. Automated tools may recommend but may not independently determine those decisions.

As participation develops, additional contributors may assume editorial review and decision-making responsibilities. The process for selecting and authorizing those participants will be documented and made public as the Commons develops.

6. Current Best Fit, Not Institutional Certainty

Inclusion in the curated glossary means that a definition represents the current best fit based on available understanding, evidence, and documented examination.

It does not constitute an institutional guarantee of correctness, completeness, universal acceptance, or permanence.

Accepted definitions remain subject to challenge, correction, refinement, replacement, or retirement.

Editorial agreement establishes a usable current reference. It does not require unanimity or extinguish documented disagreement.

7. Reconsideration and Transparency

Contributors may question editorial decisions and offer new arguments, evidence, or interpretations.

Material revisions must preserve the history of definitions, decisions, and attribution.

The founding publication, Describing the Environment We Are Trying to Govern, v1.0.2, remains a fixed historical baseline, independently preserved on Zenodo.

8. Proportionate Governance

The editorial process must remain as simple as possible while providing for meaningful examination, attribution, evidentiary discipline, and documentation of consequential decisions.

Administrative requirements must not create unnecessary barriers to participation.

Automated assistance must remain subordinate to the Commons' editorial principles and human decision authority.

The editorial process itself remains open to criticism, revision, and simplification.

Our objective is shared understanding, not shared conclusions.

Evidence Locker provisional publication

Effective October 30, 2026, the founding steward authorizes publication and routine maintenance of provisional Evidence Locker records without individual human approval of every update. Each public record must identify its source and supporting location, attribute claims, state material limitations, distinguish publication from independent verification, and preserve case identity and source dependencies. AI assistance does not turn a provider report into independent corroboration.

Routine editorial checks may be performed under this standing authority. Material disputes, uncertain provenance, privacy or rights concerns, and proposed changes to the founding glossary require further review. The initial collection is explicitly provisional and not independently verified by the Commons. Stable record IDs, dated releases, and correction routes support subsequent qualification, correction, or withdrawal.

This policy concerns provisional evidence records. The existing authorization requirement for inclusion in the curated glossary remains applicable to glossary definitions. Third-party source preservation and redistribution are separate permission decisions.
